Daily adjustment rules

Each morning, say "today" with your Oura readiness, sleep hours, and Garmin Training Readiness. Strava and your calendars are read automatically. The answer is one session that fits a real gap in the day.

  1. Green (Oura ≥ 75, sleep ≥ 6.5 h, Garmin ≥ 50): do the planned session.
  2. Yellow (Oura 60–74, or sleep 5–6.5 h, or Garmin 25–49): keep the session, drop to Z2, cap at 45 min. No tests on yellow days.
  3. Red (Oura under 60, sleep under 5 h, Garmin under 25, resting HR 7+ over baseline, or a night up with a kid): 20-minute walk or rest. Nothing is made up tomorrow; the week just gets smaller.
  4. Calendar collision: move the session to another open slot the same day (5am, lunch, 8pm) before moving it to another day. Keep one rest day. Never stack long ride and long run back to back in the rebuild block.
  5. Missed session: it is gone. Weekly hours never rise more than 10% over last week to compensate.
  6. Two yellow or red days in a row: cut the rest of the week by a third and flag it in the weekly summary.
  7. Heat: any run starting above 85°F is Z1 only or moved.
  8. Alcohol the night before: the day is yellow at best, whatever Oura says.

Alcohol

This is part of the training plan, not a side quest. The ring will show the effect on resting heart rate, HRV, and readiness within two to three weeks, and that trend gets shown back to you every Sunday.

  1. Zero drinks the night before Saturday and Sunday sessions and before any test.
  2. Cap: 3 per week in the rebuild block, 2 in base, 1 or fewer from the build onward. Last six weeks before any race: zero.
  3. Dry days are reported in the weekly check-in and tracked next to training hours.
  4. A lapse is data, not failure. Two lapses in a week means we talk about the trigger: Friday, a TIG event, bedtime.
